One thing I've been playing with is an alternative physics model for a substance that has novel physical properties and
may allow for some deeply weird shenanigans.

A core conceit I wanted to play with was the idea of 'diegetic XP', that is -- experience points are a physical
substance ("Vesper"[^1]) that players must get back to some safe place in order to consume it to level up.

Also it explodes, because it's more fun if it explodes.

I initially just gave it properties based on rule-of-cool. Vesper is released when you defeat an enemy, it's stored in
tanks, and it floats in air despite being easily moved (i.e., it has weird interactions with gravity). I arbitrarily
decided 1L of Vesper was equivalent to a L3 Fireball (the base level), and handwaved detonation whenever it came up.

So far so simple, but then I got to thinking about _how_ Vesper could do this. I also knew I wanted to model a vesper
ecosystem. Vesper as a diegetic substance means it needs to interact with regular matter somehow, and I'd already
violated some laws of physics with it's stated properties so I was in a bind.

It turns out, though, if you don't care if the physics are _real_ in the sense that they don't model _actual_ physics,
and instead are willing to venture into the world of homebrew, you would just need to make Vesper out of something that
already existed, was relatively abundant, and maybe had some pre-existing weirdness with gravity.

In (real) physics, particles generally aim to find the 'rest' state, the one that minimizes its energy; but they can
sometimes 'get stuck' in an excited state for a while. We see this in the stars, literally. Stars are made of trapped
(by gravity) particles undergoing violent fusion and being pushed to the surface, _slowly_. A photon, in some sense,
[escapes the core of the Sun](https://astronomy.stackexchange.com/a/51101) only after a wait of 100,000 years[^2]. Or
taken as a whole we can regard them as particles that themselves will take many billions of years to burn out to their
lowest energy states (dispersion and 'heat death'). In _Eret_, Vesper is like this, but the range over which it must be
spread is much larger for it to fully 'go out'. In _Eret_, Vesper decays to Dark Matter.
