# Irrefutable

A thing I cannot disbelieve. That is, a fact so fundamental I cannot be convinced to hold its negation.

An example might be the fact that I percieve at all. Any attempt to convince me that I am not percieving is its own
refutation, as I must percieve in order to be convinced.

All definitions are irrefutable if accepted, scoped to the relevant context.

Most of intuitive logic is irrefutable to me. I can't really 'believe' it does hold, I can work with it symbollically,
but only if I dissociate from the warped semantics it would imply.

# Beliefs

## Positive Belief

A Positive Belief is a money down bet, an assertion without need for further justification, an obvious fact, an axiom.
For instance, I am a philosophical monist, by which I mean I accept as fact without further justification that there is
only one kind of _stuff_ ("first-substance") I am capable of percieving, and further that this is the only kind of
_stuff_ there is (there is no "second-substance" out of which stuff may be made, nor any "nth-substance").

## Negative Belief

A Negative Belief is a fold, an assertion of impossibility or boundary, a nonobvious lie, a
well-foundedness/well-formedness requirement (a "coaxiom"). A positive belief establishes a claim about something, a
negative belief cedes the right to claim anything about a class of things.

For instance. I am a first-substance atheist, second-substance agnostic. That is to say, I have a positive belief that
there is no thing approximating a deity as described by any religion I have heard describe the concept made out of the
same stuff as me. I have a negative belief that we can answer questions about it if my belief about philosophical monism
is wrong.

## The role of beliefs

Beliefs are bets. Unlike irrefutables, I generally _can_ make myself believe the opposite thing. Indeed, at various
points in my life I have had opposite beliefs (I was a second-substance theist before becoming a second-substance
agnostic; I was a dualist before I was a monist). I may hold opposing beliefs in the future as well.

Beliefs are necessary but evil. Any structure built on belief necessarily needs those beliefs to hold over time, and
since beliefs are fundamentally guesses, they are sensitive to those guesses being wrong.
