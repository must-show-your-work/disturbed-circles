Dear Εὐκλείδης,

You're dead. I hate to break it to me, but that means you'll never read this letter. Not only that, I'm not even going
to take the time to write it in Ἑλληνική for you. That's because this is a narrative conceit and not some sort of break
with reality. I hate writing to no one in particular, though I sometimes do, I much prefer picking characters who can
embody some sort of archetype that maybe can learn over time. Sometimes it makes sense to show it to a student, or a
teacher, or a colleague. Sometimes it is helpful if those are real people, sometimes it's easier when they aren't.

My purpose here is to start to work out, informally, an alternative way to think about your subject, Στοιχεῖα. You are
the name we most associate with what we call Plane Geometry, so much that we named it after you. That's a pretty big
deal, not a lot of people get entire subjects named after them, certainly not one as wide as yours.[^1]



----


[^1]: Growing up, the lady who ran the pizza joint we preferred was a Greek lady named Maria. She would frequently use a
    phrase, "Prouder than Euclid's Mom" -- sometimes it was other famous Greek people, usually it was Euclid. When I
    told her I was going to school for math she gave me a free pizza.
