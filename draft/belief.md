I've been thinking a bunch about what I _believe_. I grew up in a sea of belief. Everyone around me _believed_ stuff
really hard and all the time. I wasn't immune to this belief, I shared it. I drank deep from the communion cup and
scarfed down the leftover bread and grape juice once a month; God was not a god of wastefulness.

I've been thinking a bunch about what I _feel is true_, as that seemed to be the majority of what it was that mattered
in the currents I found myself in. I'd watch as the Spirit took the men and women of the Church I Grew Up In and they
would shout their 'Amens' and 'Hallelujahs' and 'Preaches' at the Pastor. I'd wonder whether what they had would come
over me too. I don't know that I ever wished for it explicitly, I definitely never prayed for it, but I wondered about
it. I am very curious.

In my life, I've found a few things I regard as _irrefutables_. These are sort of informal axioms. I take them as given
in all applicable (and most unapplicable) situations. This not to say that they're _actually_ irrefutable, just that "I
cannot find a way not to assume these even when I try very hard." They're sort of singularities of whatever function
specifies the things I most fundamentally believe. Sometimes I find ways to resolve them -- others I've assumed were for
a while until I found out otherwise. Some are straightforward charges, "Find ways to be kind." Others are well known
'laws' -- rules for parsimony, rules for concision -- Occam and Hofstadter and Conway and so on. Some were falsely
placed there by others, or more accurately, preconceptions were taught to me that said "These things are true, just like
those other things, no need to inspect yourself, just trust us." It took me a long time to find time and space and
safety to study them with the proper tools.

I don't claim this is the "right" way to do things. I don't think there is a "right" way to do things, things are never
that simple. Rather, this is the way _I_ percieve things and how I identify the things I "believe" for lack of a better
term. These "irrefutables" are things I "believe", as far as I can tell.

I guess amongst the more basic things I find irrefutable is mathematics itself. I trust it in a way I saw others have
faith in Christ or The Church or the Concept of a Higher Power that Personally and Directly Cared about Them. As I got
older though, got safer, got distance from the dogma -- I found that my faith in mathematics and my faith in Christ were
of deeply different qualities. Christ-faith wasn't built out of anything but the authority of the people around me who
were responsible for my safety. It was taught to me as if they'd found something, as if they discovered something
they all determined was irrefutable; but in reality it was a sandcastle they had been meticulously trenching and
tweaking and repairing as the tide rolled in. It would never survive, but maybe if they _believed_ enough, they could
keep part of it.

They would preach it on Sundays and Wednesdays and they would wear their faith like a badge during the week. Spiritual
Staties on patrol, wearing holier-than-thou like a uniform and forgetting the admonition against pharisticial piety.
They did not pray in secret.

I did do a lot of praying in secret. I'd stay up late most nights, I had frequent trouble sleeping. I'd stare at the
ceiling, think about math or the Bible or _Lord of the Rings_. I think I started noticing issues when I was in my early
teens. Stuff stuck out to me. Christ was only in the ground for 36 hours, that's not three days. He has two different
geneaologies -- which isn't a problem except for all the literalism and inerrancy stuff we believed. God is supposed to
be immutable, but immutibility is a limitation, and he's also supposed to be all powerful, so how could he accept a
limitation if he definitionally has none? What does omniscience even mean? Does God know all the numbers? How does he
tell the Reals apart?

Does God have to imagine $\sqrt(-1)$?

These aren't deep questions now, they're mostly answered by denying the stupid idea of inerrancy, and realizing the
bible is a book and the God I grew up with wasn't anything more than the shadowplay put on by simple, mean men. I'm an
agnostic-atheist, which is to say, I don't believe in a literal entity one could ascribe godhood to, and I also think
the question is immaterial in some sense. It doesn't matter if 'god' exists, if they do, there is nothing I can do about
it, and if they don't, there is nothing I can do about it. "If you have no ability to respond, you do not have a
responsibility."

They were deep questions then, though. I dove into my beliefs while others stayed on the surface. I had nothing better
to do on the long humid nights of summer, listening to the woods outside my window. Peepers on the pool cover, flaps of
bat wings; then mourning doves and sunrise. Those questions took me all night and most of morning to work through, but
they brought me to the first things I truly believed in.

"I percieve." I'd learned the Cogito by accident, I looked up Descartes on the internet, and found a Dr. Math article
about him. My textbook focused on the coordinate system, but Dr. Math mentioned his work as a philosopher. A new idea
landed with the word. I hadn't realized philosophy was still a thing at that point. I must have been 15? I saw "I think,
therefore I am." I had questions, I always had questions. I wondered what thinking _was_ on some level, like -- what was
I doing? Was it the same as everyone else? I found a way around solipsism thanks to some random lady on the internet, I
remember asking in RPG World Online, an absolutely ancient thing that felt ancient at the time. A VB6-powered...
chatroom with extra steps is probably the best description. I asked (I have forgetten her name, but not her kindness)
her if she had heard of this Descartes thing and if other people were real. I was not a shy kid when it came to my
questions, only in everything else.
